The world just outside our door

Myrtle Creek
is our Barrow's Green.

The South Umpqua River runs along the western edge of town as it always has. The railroad grade cuts through the hills. The old mill site is a park now, with young myrtle trees growing where the loading dock used to be.

The church bell rings on Sundays and, twice now, without explanation. The Homestead sits at the center of it all, on the oldest street in town, with the kettle on.

The people of Main Street

Every homestead
has its regulars.

ER

Eula Rafferty

The Regular

Arrives the first Saturday of every month. Takes a slow loop counterclockwise. Never leaves without buying something small.

She once turned down a serious offer on her farmhouse. She has never spoken of it.

CW

Clement Wharry

The Grower

Third-generation farmer, twelve acres east of town. Brings bundles each season. Leaves before anyone can make a fuss over him.

He does not have a phone. He is reachable by showing up.

PB

Philippa Breck

The Neighbor

Eleven years on Main Street. Knows everyone by name and most by their coffee order. Stops in Wednesday mornings.

There is a mural dispute with the city. She calls it "historically accurate." The mural remains.

OV

Oswald Vane

The Maker

Nobody knows where his workshop is. He makes furniture from salvaged Oregon timber. Each piece takes as long as it takes.

He keeps every letter he's ever received in a cigar box. He never mentions them.

Millsite Park · Behind the Homestead

Where the old mill
stood, we gather.

Just behind the Homestead, where the old timber mill once ran, there is a park now. On certain mornings, when the mist sits low, old-timers say you can still hear the mill. Most likely it is the river. Most likely.

Spring

The Millsite Awakening

The first gathering of the year, held when the myrtle trees begin to leaf. Makers, local growers, long tables, the kettle on.

Summer

The Main Street Evening

Shops open late, lanterns between the buildings. Philippa Breck organizing something that will go perfectly or become a story told for years. Usually both.

Fall

The Harvest Table

A long dinner in Millsite Park. Clement brings what the farm produced. Oswald builds the table. Eula arrives first and leaves last.

Winter

The Homestead Holiday

Candles in the windows, the shop warm against the December dark. The bell ringing without explanation. The whole town finds its way to Main Street.
